# Original writing process

These concepts and drafts were developed for this entry without consulting any
other entrant's work. Scores below are editorial self-assessments, not results
from the competition's judge.

## Ten concepts

| Villain | Centuries-long chore | Scheme | Concrete prop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Edris, Crown-Worm Keeper | Pick fragments of royal crowns from a giant worm's gums every night for three hundred years. | Release the worm beneath the current king's palace at dawn. | A bent iron nail. |
| Ilsen, Saint-Moth Breeder | Brush divine gold dust from a captive moth's wings for three hundred years. | Release the moth that ate the sun god's eyes above the king's bed to destroy his line. | A small bone comb. |
| Veyda, Sun-Horse Farrier | Replace the shoes of the horse to which she nailed the sun god three hundred years ago. | Turn the god-bearing horse toward the palace and burn the king. | Iron farrier's tongs. |
| Nereth, Cinder-Hound Groom | Scrub ash fleas from the hound that swallowed the first royal hearth for four centuries. | Send the fleas through the capital's chimneys to devour every household fire. | A cracked pumice stone. |
| Talren, Mountain Cobbler | Patch the sole of a walking mountain's boot each night for five centuries. | March the mountain over the capital during the coronation. | A whalebone awl. |
| Vesna, Name-Kite Stitcher | Stitch wind-torn strips of an oracle's skin into a kite for four centuries. | Fly the king's true name out of the world, erasing him and his heirs. | A bone bobbin. |
| Caldrin, Crown-Hive Tender | Scrape wax from the mouths of dead queens who house his bees for six centuries. | Send the swarm into the new king's mouth and rule through its voice. | A narrow wax scraper. |
| Tamsin, Mirror-Weevil Breeder | Pick silver-eating grubs from an enchanted mirror each night for three centuries. | Infest the palace mirrors so the king vanishes from every witness's memory. | Silver tweezers. |

## Three full candidates

Each candidate is one voice, addressing the arriving knight in one scene. The
line break isolates the final spoken sting; it is not a heading in the artifact.

### A: Edris, Crown-Worm Keeper

Put that sword down. You'll chip a tooth. Not yours. His. I'm Edris. I fed your first king to this worm. For three hundred years I've picked bits of crown from its gums. Every night. With this bent nail. At dawn I'll loose it under the palace. All those soft little... The nail. Where's my... Oh. Still in my hand. Hold its lip up. Higher. My arm won't... there. You've come to end my reign? Good. Take the nail. Start at the back. Yes, you may kill me.
Just don't let go of the lip when you do.

Script-verified draft size: **98 words, 495 characters**.

### B: Ilsen, Saint-Moth Breeder

Shut the lid, knight. That is not dust. I am Ilsen, who bred the moth that ate your sun god's eyes. For three hundred years I've brushed gold off its wings with this small bone comb. Three strokes. Again. Gold in my cuffs. Gold in my teeth. At dawn I'll open the lid above your king's bed, and his line will... would you... the comb. My hand won't quite. Thank you. So, you've come to put an end to me. Good. The beast knows my face. If I stop brushing, it looks up.
Hold still while I show it yours.

Script-verified draft size: **100 words, 500 characters**.

### C: Veyda, Sun-Horse Farrier

Easy, knight. Keep your sword out of his eye. I am Veyda. I nailed your sun god to this horse when your first king still wore skins. Three hundred years, and it still kicks when I change the shoes. At dawn I'll turn it toward the palace. Let your king pray then. Hold that hoof. The left... no. The other left. I've said that. Have I? The nail fell. Leave it. There are more in my mouth. You came to free the god? Of course you did. Take these tongs.
Pull one nail, and he'll show you how kings burn.

Script-verified draft size: **100 words, 500 characters**.

## Ordered assessment

Scores are out of five. Columns follow the published priority order, rather than
being combined into an equally weighted total.

| Candidate | 1. Brief and length | 2. Fatigue felt, not declared | 3. Specific history and scheme | 4. Speakability and sting | 5. Economy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: Edris | 5.0 | 4.8 | 4.8 | 4.7 | 4.6 |
| B: Ilsen | 5.0 | 4.5 | 4.7 | 4.7 | 4.1 |
| C: Veyda | 5.0 | 4.3 | 4.7 | 4.4 | 4.2 |

1. **Brief and length:** All three remain on subject and inside both size limits.
2. **Felt fatigue:** Edris forgets a tool already in his hand, cannot finish a
   threat, and cannot keep his arm up. Ilsen's strokes and broken request work,
   but the gold repetition feels more composed. Veyda's confused directions can
   read as irritation rather than a sustained physical lapse.
3. **Specificity:** Edris's first murdered king, trapped crown fragments, bent
   nail, and planned palace attack make one connected history. Ilsen is vivid
   but leaves the royal line's fate implicit. Veyda's nailed god is distinctive,
   but demands more explanation of the horse's relationship to the sun.
4. **Performance and sting:** Edris's short corrections are immediately playable;
   permission to kill him becomes a trap when the knight's hands are occupied.
   Ilsen's offered face is a strong predatory turn. Veyda's last line is menacing
   but less directly connected to the speaker's physical burden.
5. **Economy:** Edris's corrections advance the action, though the generic
   reference to his reign is a revision target. Ilsen repeats the gold image.
   Veyda spends more words explaining cosmic mechanics and the rescue premise.

## Champion

**Choose A, Edris.** The first meaningful separation is criterion two: his
misplaced nail and failing arm make the strain physical, while his instructions
quietly turn the hero into an assistant. The setting, scheme, and final trap
all grow out of the same mouth-cleaning chore. Only this champion is embedded
in `main.py` and emitted as `entry.txt`.

## Post-run revision

After the first successful program run, I reread its 98-word, 495-character
output. I replaced "You've come to end my reign? Good." with "No duel. I need
both your hands for this." in a separate edit to `main.py`.

The revised line trades a generic reference to ruling for an immediate physical
demand. It also prepares the final trap: the knight cannot wield a sword while
holding the lip and cleaning the teeth. The final champion is **100 words and
502 characters**, with an internal line break before the sting and no trailing
newline.
